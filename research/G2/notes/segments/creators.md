# Early-adopter segment: creators who could run book clubs or listening clubs

All sources accessed 2026-09-28 unless noted. "Page date" is the date shown on the page. Items marked *(search snippet only)* came from a search-result summary and the page itself was not fetched; treat them as lower confidence. Research budget was about 17 tool calls, so coverage is broad but shallow; see Gaps.

## 1. Jobs: what creators want from a club, and what members want

### Takeaway
Creators use clubs as a **paid-tier perk and retention device** that gives people a reason to come back between episodes or posts (Patreon book club and Discord bundles, Substack paid-only Zoom calls and threads). Members want to talk with other readers across ages and places, and to have scheduled moments together (monthly pick, calls, walks). I found no survey that measures "club" demand directly. The evidence is named examples plus platform claims about engagement.

### Cited Findings
- Lemonade Stand (podcast) was "initially supported entirely through Patreon subscriptions". Its perks were bonus episodes, a private Discord server **and a book club**. Bloomberg reported (cited by Wikipedia) that by Oct 2025 it earned more than **$58,000/month** on Patreon, and Vox Media then took over sales and distribution — [Wikipedia: Lemonade Stand (podcast)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemonade_Stand_(podcast)) (secondary source citing Bloomberg; page undated)
- Substack's own post on book clubs lists paid-subscriber models: Laura Kennedy caps Zoom meetings at 15 attendees, and Eleanor Mills's club is for paying subscribers only. Features in use are discussion threads, author Q&As, polls for picking the book, subscriber discount codes, and Eventbrite for events — [Substack: "Your next book club is taking place on Substack"](https://on.substack.com/p/book-clubs) (page date 2023-07-18, **older than 2024**)
- Emily's Walking Book Club (Substack) combines London Heath walks, Monday Zoom calls and threads: "50+ walkers, 10–15 Zoom participants, hundreds of thread comments". What members get, in Emily Rhodes's words: "Young folk in their 20s chat to people in their 90s" — [Substack book clubs post](https://on.substack.com/p/book-clubs) (2023-07-18)
- Paid-subscriber club formats on Substack in 2025: the Reading and Purpose Nonfiction Book Club offers paid subscribers online chats and Zoom meetings (discussions from Jan 2025). The Read and Think Deeply Book Club 2025 runs weekly summaries plus member chat — [readingandpurpose.substack.com](https://readingandpurpose.substack.com/p/the-reading-and-purpose-nonfiction); [ryandhall.substack.com](https://ryandhall.substack.com/p/introducing-the-read-and-think-deeply) *(search snippets only)*
- Katherine May's True Stories Book Club uses **readalong recordings** and chat threads (e.g. *Braiding Sweetgrass*). This is the closest existing format to "listen together and react" — [katherinemay.substack.com](https://katherinemay.substack.com/t/true-stories-book-club) *(search snippet only)*
- Spotify says listeners who use interactive features such as comments are on average **4x more likely to return to a show within 30 days** and spend twice as many hours per month listening. Spotify rolled out podcast comments in July 2024, replacing Q&A — [TechCrunch 2024-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/chasing-youtube-spotify-adds-comments-to-podcasts); [Podnews](https://podnews.net/update/spotify-app-and-comments) *(search snippets only; this is Spotify's own claim)*
- Patreon's "State of Create" (page date 2025-02-19): creators report falling reach as social platforms deprioritize the follow function, and are moving toward owning their audience connection. Almost 90% would still recommend being a creator. The news post gives no community or club metrics — [Patreon News: State of Create in 2025](https://news.patreon.com/articles/state-of-create)
- Kit (formerly ConvertKit) creator survey (1,000 creators; **2024 report**): one in four full-time creators gets the best audience engagement through email. No club or community question was surfaced — [Kit 2024 State of the Creator Economy](https://kit.com/reports/creator-economy-2024) *(search snippet only; I did not find a 2025 or 2026 edition)*

### Inferences
- The creator job to be done is "a reason to pay and a reason to come back between releases". A club is sold as a perk bundled with bonus content and Discord. Spoken Margins would slot in as the *activity* inside that perk, not as the community host.
- For audio creators, "hear what other members said at this exact moment" is a new kind of engagement that comments and chat can't express. Evidence that creators *want* moment-anchored discussion is indirect: readalong recordings, and Spotify's timestamped comments push.

### Gaps
- No quantitative survey of how many podcasters or newsletter writers run clubs, or of club churn and retention effects. Patreon's full report (stateofcreate.co) was not read.
- No member-side survey of what club members want. The claims above are anecdotal.

## 2. Tools used now (with numbers)

### Takeaway
Book-club-specific apps are large but text- and book-centric (Fable: 3M users and 100k+ clubs; Bookclubs: 75k+ clubs claimed). Creator communities mostly live in general tools (Discord, Patreon, Substack chat and threads, Zoom). The podcast platforms offer per-episode comments, not anchored group listening. Geneva, one dedicated club app, is gone.

### Cited Findings
- **Fable**: 3 million users and **100,000+ book clubs**; acquired by Everand (Scribd Inc.) in June 2025. Creator-hosted clubs: Ayman Chaudhary's Spicy Romance Book Club (46,500 members), Mychal Threets (10,700), Kierra Lewis's Cousin Harry Book Club (6,500), plus LeVar Burton and Sean Astin. Free and premium tiers; also sells club hosting for businesses and schools. Raised $7.25M seed plus $20M. Early 2025 incident: its AI year-end summaries produced "offensive and racially insensitive content" and the feature was removed — [Publishers Weekly 2025-06-10](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/97975-everand-acquires-online-book-club-platform-fable.html)
- Fable is on both iOS and Android — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fable-track-discuss-books/id1488170618); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=co.fable.fable&hl=en_US) (listings exist; details not fetched)
- **Bookclubs** (bookclubs.com): runs on the web plus iOS and Android, all free. Features are scheduling and RSVPs, member management, messaging, book polls and discussion guides. It lists "content creators" among its audiences. The About page says it serves "thousands of book clubs" and "approached 100,000 members", and its origin story dates it to 2015 — [Bookclubs About](https://bookclubs.com/about) (undated). **Conflicts:** search summaries cite "over 1M members" and "75K+ book clubs" and a 2019 founding — [search result](https://bookclubs.com/) *(snippet only, unverified)*
- **Geneva**: acquired by Bumble in 2024. Bumble said in Q2 2025 that Geneva "had not generated any revenue as of June 30". It was shut down with the Bumble BFF relaunch (Sept 2025) and members moved to BFF Groups, whose discovery was due Feb 2026 — [TechCrunch 2025-09-18](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building/); [TechCrunch 2024-05-20](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/20/bumble-buys-community-building-app-geneva-to-expand-further-into-friendships/)
- **Patreon + Discord**: common podcast pattern. Lemonade Stand (above), and Comic Book Club offers a private Discord, bonus shows and ad-free feeds — [Patreon: Comic Book Club](https://www.patreon.com/cw/comicbookclub) *(snippet only)*. The Book Club Review podcast offers extended Patreon episodes — [thebookclubreview.co.uk](https://www.thebookclubreview.co.uk/) *(snippet only)*
- **Substack**: threads, chat, Zoom and polls (see §1). A community-run "Substack Book Group Directory" updated monthly exists — [Footnotes and Tangents](https://footnotesandtangents.substack.com/i/146107661/archive) *(snippet only)*
- **Spotify comments**: launched July 2024, replaced Q&A. Comments are private by default and the creator chooses which appear — [TechCrunch 2024-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/chasing-youtube-spotify-adds-comments-to-podcasts); upgrades in June 2025 — [Radio Ink 2025-06-02](https://radioink.com/2025/06/02/spotify-upgrades-podcast-discovery-and-comments-features/) *(snippets only)*
- **margin.fm**: iOS-only (iPhone and iPad), Spotify-only podcast notes. Press and hold to pause Spotify, speak, and resume; the note is timestamped and transcribed on-device with Apple Speech. Free as of Sept 2026, Pro (cloud sync, better transcription) announced. **No social or sharing features described.** Competitors it names: Snipd, Apple Notes, Notion, Obsidian, Otter.ai, Pocket Casts, Airr (discontinued) — [margin.fm: best podcast note-taking apps 2026](https://margin.fm/posts/01-best-podcast-note-taking-apps-2026) (2026, self-published by the vendor)

### Inferences
- Nobody found here offers "shared, moment-anchored audio reactions for a group". Fable and Bookclubs are text and book-level; Spotify comments are episode-level and public-ish; margin.fm is solo. This is Spoken Margins' opening, but also a sign the job is unproven.
- Geneva shows that a general "clubs app" struggled to earn revenue. A club feature is safer as a layer on top of where creators already are (Patreon, Substack, Discord) than as a new place to gather.
- All the book-club incumbents ship on Android and web. Creators expect every member to be able to join.

### Gaps
- No verified numbers for Discord, Circle, Goodreads groups, YouTube community posts or Apple Podcasts social features in a club context (not researched within budget).
- Fable's club features (whether audiobook or podcast support exists) were not confirmed.

## 4. Named examples of podcast or newsletter clubs and how they run

### Cited Findings
- **Lemonade Stand**: Patreon tier with bonus episodes, Discord and book club; more than $58k/month by Oct 2025 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemonade_Stand_(podcast))
- **Roxane Gay's Audacious Book Club** (Substack): monthly announcement, then discussion threads through the month — [Substack 2023-07-18](https://on.substack.com/p/book-clubs)
- **Emily's Walking Book Club**: walks, Zoom and threads — same source
- **Laura Kennedy**: Zoom capped at 15; **Eleanor Mills**: paid-only — same source
- **Katherine May, True Stories Book Club**: readalong recordings and chat — [katherinemay.substack.com](https://katherinemay.substack.com/t/true-stories-book-club) *(snippet)*
- **Service95 Book Club** (Dua Lipa): launched June 2023, monthly pick with interviews, essays and playlists — [Wikipedia: Service95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service95) *(snippet)*
- **Fable creator clubs**: Spicy Romance (46.5k), Mychal Threets (10.7k), Cousin Harry (6.5k) — [PW 2025-06-10](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/97975-everand-acquires-online-book-club-platform-fable.html)
- **Celebrity Memoir Book Club** and **Omnibus**: book-centric podcasts whose format is hosts discussing a book, not listeners discussing together — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_Memoir_Book_Club) *(snippet)*

### Inferences
- The common rhythm is **monthly pick → async thread → one live call**. A Spoken Margins pilot fits into the async slot: members drop spoken Margins on the episode or article during the month, and the creator plays the best ones on the live call or in a bonus episode.

## 5. Product gaps that would stop them

### Cited Findings
- iPhone is about 1 in 4 active smartphones worldwide in 2025 — [Counterpoint via 9to5Mac 2026-02-10](https://9to5mac.com/2026/02/10/iphone-now-accounts-for-nearly-one-in-four-active-smartphones-worldwide-report/). Apple had 69% of US smartphone **sales** in Q4 2025 (this is a sales share, not installed base) — [Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/q4-2025-apple-share-grows-to-historic-levels-as-us-smartphone-market-up-1-yoy) *(snippets)*
- Competitors creators already use (Fable, Bookclubs) are on web plus iOS plus Android — sources in §2.
- margin.fm is also iOS-only, with no web or Android — [margin.fm](https://margin.fm/posts/01-best-podcast-note-taking-apps-2026)

### Inferences (not sourced; product reasoning)
- **iOS 26+ only, TestFlight only**: any club with international or Android members loses part of its members. Probably acceptable in the US, where iPhone sales share is high, but not for "the whole club joins".
- **Packets are files, not links**: a creator can't post one URL in Discord or Substack. Each member has to download and import a file, and every new Margin means a new file. This is the biggest barrier to a club *rhythm*.
- **No live group feature (co-op)**: members can't hear each other's new Margins without re-exchanging packets.
- **No creator dashboard**: no view of who took part or which moments drew reactions, which is the engagement proof creators pitch to paid tiers.
- **Rights**: sharing a creator's *own* episode is fine, but clubs built on third-party articles or PDFs raise the question of whether packets carry source text or transcripts. Not researched here; flag for legal and product.
- **TestFlight onboarding**: a 10-person invite is doable; a 1,000-member Patreon is not.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on creator attitudes to iOS-only tools or file-based sharing.
- No copyright guidance on sharing transcripts or excerpts was researched.

## 6. Smallest two-week test of real interest

### Inferences (recommendation, not sourced)
- **Run one concierge pilot, recruited from a creator who already runs a club.** Target a Substack readalong or book-club writer (from the Footnotes and Tangents directory, or Katherine May-style readalong writers) or a Patreon book podcast. Ask for 8–12 iPhone members through TestFlight.
  - Week 1: the owner prepares one source (the creator's own episode or article, which avoids rights issues). The creator records 3–5 seed Margins and exports a packet. The owner posts it in the creator's existing channel (Substack chat or Discord). Members import, listen, and add their own Margins, which they send back as packets. The owner merges them by hand into one round-up packet.
  - Week 2: redistribute the combined packet, and the creator plays the best Margins in a bonus episode or on the live call.
  - **Success signals:** at least 50% of invited members import; at least 30% record at least one Margin; the creator asks to do it again or offers it as a paid perk. **Failure signals:** drop-off at TestFlight or packet import, or members replying in chat instead of in Margins.
- A cheaper fallback: the owner runs a club themselves on one public-domain or own-rights source, with 10 iPhone friends. This measures the mechanics but not creator demand.
- In parallel, send 10–20 cold emails to club-running creators with a 60-second demo video. A reply rate above about 20% is a signal of creator interest independent of the build.

### Gaps
- No benchmarks were found for pilot conversion rates in creator-club tools; the thresholds above are judgment calls.
