# Co-op packet and playback: failed owner gate and incomplete repair

September 24, 2026. This is an account of the attempted three-person podcast Margin exchange, the failed owner test, and the engineering stopping point. It is not a release handoff or a claim that the app now works end to end.

## The requested experience

Cole shares a packet with 22 Margins on a Jensen Huang podcast. Alex and Blair each reply to an exact Cole revision. Casey replies to the exact Alex and Blair revisions. Importing a returned packet should bring the podcast into the Library automatically. Cole should be able to find unfamiliar Margins without replaying all 22 of his own, hear a selected reply with useful parent context, skip or finish it, and continue the podcast at the appropriate source position. The podcast scrubber should remain visible, the auto-play setting should mean what it says, and the flow should be demonstrable in a screen recording with actual app audio. Cole's recordings and the podcast must remain intact.

That complete experience has **not** been demonstrated. The product-owner gate failed, and no trustworthy end-to-end audiovisual recording was delivered.

## What happened

1. **Strong component evidence was mistaken for readiness.** The earlier podcast-anchor candidate passed fast checks, 482/482 app tests, 11/11 adapters, 1/1 speech canary, 16/16 UI routes, generic iOS builds, a zero-false-confident held-out anchor benchmark, and a focused real AVFoundation export test on macOS. The newest 483-test app bundle then stalled before XCTest connected, so 0/483 ran and its new iOS export test was unexecuted. Those results supported particular implementation claims, not the requested import-and-listen journey. [Original verification handoff](../podcast-anchor-reliability-2026-09-23/full-verification-2026-09-24.md).
2. **The first packet plan missed a core relationship.** The then-current conversation contract allowed one parent per reply, while Casey needed two exact parent revisions. The first Sol instruction packet correctly stopped rather than faking a second link in spoken text. The owner authorized the extension. Conversation v2, graph/import behavior, and three Chatterbox Nano response packets were later built and checked through the production archive/importer. A later current-input app suite passed 484/484, while its adapter run stalled before connecting (0/11). The packet handoff's initial readiness language was withdrawn after the owner test. [Two-parent history](two-parent-blocker.md); [packet handoff](owner-packet-handoff.md).
3. **The owner test exposed the real failure.** On a physical iPhone 17 Pro running iOS 27, the owner reported that Skim stranded podcast progress at 19:08 and that local and shared Margins did not play. The owner rejected the Cancel Skim control. Device diagnostics showed an accepted source seek to 19:08.038 and a `playing` snapshot while media time stayed fixed through repeated observations. The then-current Skim path waited for a 120-second source lead-in to reach its destination, with no no-progress timeout, so the Margin could remain gated behind a stalled podcast. That evidence explains the blocked Skim path; it does not prove every recording was silent. The owner later clarified that one of his own Margins **was** playing, but was picked up by a video-call microphone. [Failed-device incident](failed-device-run/incident.md).
4. **Follow-up work found more failures and implemented partial repairs.** The app was changed to start an explicitly selected Margin directly and bound optional context waits; Cancel Skim was removed. A later UI pass restored the podcast scrubber, added Margin progress and skip-to-end, removed the oversized Continue Source control, separated explicit Listen from cue auto-play, and excluded locally authored Margins from New Margins while retaining exact parent context. The packet-import path was repaired so a publisher-page resolver failure could fall back to the packet's validated podcast enclosure; agent-operated simulator exploration then imported the real Alex packet and created the episode without a separate Add Source action. [Incident and import correction](failed-device-run/incident.md); [UX checkpoint](ux-repair-2026-09-24/checkpoint.md).
5. **The last continuation repaired three reproduced simulator defects but did not close the flow.** A failed replacement download had hidden a complete 106,377,881-byte saved episode; the coordinator now retains and recovers that file. Imported audio files existed, but stored absolute paths named an old simulator container; audio-path resolution now rebases them to the current container. Skim from a restored paused session failed because no media player existed yet; it now stages the source bookmark for the next start. The host fast plan and generic simulator/device builds passed. New app regression source for the download and audio-path cases was not executed because the owner had stopped scripted simulator testing. [Repair continuation](ux-repair-2026-09-24/playback-repair-continuation.md).

## What the latest simulator exploration actually showed

On the installed iOS 26.5 simulator candidate, the saved podcast clock advanced; New Margins hid Cole and showed Alex; a verified Skim moved the podcast display to Cole's 30:44 source location while Cole's separate Margin progress advanced; skip-to-end returned to podcast playback at 30:44 and the source advanced. Direct Listen opened Alex's Margin, its progress advanced and later completed. Alex's source location remained unmatched, so the app did not claim an exact podcast seek for Alex. Casey was not present in that simulator layer. This is useful visual/runtime evidence, but it is **not** a complete packet import, three-person conversation, export/reimport, physical-device recovery, or proof of audible content.

The attempted recording did not fill the gap. A window-only ScreenCaptureKit video had an AAC track that measured as silence. A second capture contained sound but recorded another Mac display and unrelated audio, so it was deleted and cannot be used as app evidence. There is no valid screen recording with confirmed Cole, Alex, and Casey app audio. [Repair continuation](ux-repair-2026-09-24/playback-repair-continuation.md).

## Why the outcome was poor

The work optimized around contract correctness and test counts before proving the owner journey on one identifiable build. The initial one-parent omission forced a scope correction after a readiness discussion. The owner then found a playback failure that the passing suites had not falsified. Subsequent fixes moved between app behavior, source acquisition, audio file resolution, UI design, and capture tooling, but the evidence never closed the full loop. Calling a packet “ready” before a real import-and-listen run was a mistake. The later decision to withhold another readiness claim was correct, but it came after the owner's failed exercise and additional usage.

The usage cost matters. The owner reported that reaching the initial readiness discussion had burned about 20% of weekly usage. Recorded account-meter checkpoints for the later implementation and repair work were 38% used at the two-target implementation entry, 44% after packet generation and handoff, 58% at the UI repair entry, 64% at that checkpoint, and 73% after the last repair continuation. These are account readings, not a precise allocation of each percentage point to this work. The 64%→73% continuation produced real repairs but still did not deliver the requested recording. The process failed the owner's cost-to-evidence expectation. [Implementation stop](implementation-stop-2026-09-24.md); [packet handoff](owner-packet-handoff.md); [UX checkpoint](ux-repair-2026-09-24/checkpoint.md); [repair continuation](ux-repair-2026-09-24/playback-repair-continuation.md).

## Honest stopping point

The broad statement “nothing was fixed” would be inaccurate: multiple reproduced defects have code repairs and bounded verification. The statement “the app is fixed” would also be inaccurate. The original physical-device failure has not been re-tested by the owner on a corrected build; the current simulator has no Casey layer; Alex has no verified source placement there; the newest app tests for the final repair were not run under the owner's simulator-test stop; the saved 22-Margin audio still has many measured long pauses; and a scoped, audible app recording does not exist. None of the complete exchange is `human_validated`.

This is the falsifiable completion bar: identify one exact candidate; import the actual three packets into an isolated recipient Library and confirm automatic podcast acquisition; show Cole, Alex, Blair, and Casey with Casey's two exact parents; capture actual app audio while navigating New Margins, direct Listen, context, skip-to-end, and a progressing podcast; record a reply and verify export/reimport; then have the owner accept the flow on a physical device. Stop at the first failure and diagnose that failure before another readiness claim. Automated checks and builds can support the candidate, but they cannot substitute for that proof. Until then, the packet and app should be treated as an unfinished, failed owner-gate candidate, with no commit, merge, physical-device installation, or distribution authorized by this record.
