# Questions for one attorney meeting (DRAFT)

Read with [privacy-policy-draft.md](privacy-policy-draft.md) and [terms-draft.md](terms-draft.md).

The list was cut from 20 questions to 6 on 2026-10-16. A question stays only if the answer turns on legal judgment we can't get from the statute, Apple's rules or a fee table. ## Questions for counsel

In priority order. The first two are where the real exposure is.

1. **Listening maps.** A phone transcribes a public podcast episode on the device and uploads the transcript. Our server passes it to an AI service and publishes a map: chapter titles, one-sentence descriptions and timestamps, like a table of contents. Is the transient server copy, and publishing the map, likely fair use? What would make a summary a derivative work instead of an index?
   - Planned changes (#1730): the transcript is never written to disk; anchors become hashes and timestamps, not quoted sentences; descriptions are capped at about 25 words, written in our own order, and rejected if they copy 8 or more words in a row.
   - Also: does the Ninth Circuit's RAM-copy rule (*MAI v. Peak*) make the server copy a fixed copy, and does that matter if the use is fair? Is the listener's transcript their own private copy, so that our part is a secondary-liability question?
   - Full transcripts are never shared (D14).
2. **Share links: the source excerpt next to a comment.** A shared Margin is the listener's own commentary on a moment in a source. The share page and the packet show that comment with a short marked quotation of the source around it, plus the title, the show and a link to the source. A link never carries the full source text or the source's audio (D15). Is a short excerpt that only gives a comment its context fair use? Please mark up these starting caps (D16):
   - after the Margin: 120 characters;
   - before the Margin: 3 times the length of the comment, at least 280 and at most 800 characters (for a spoken Margin, the transcript's length, or about 15 characters per second of audio);
   - Open: there is no total limit per link. Does a link with many Margins need one?
   - Also: if a quote went too far, would the claim be against the listener who chose it, or against us? For a very short source, such as a poem or a 300-word post, should we also cap the excerpt as a share of the source?
3. **The § 512(c) safe harbor and abuse-material reports when we can't read what we host.** Everything a link holds, the packet and the page's preview text, is encrypted on the phone (D20). The key travels only in the link, so the server stores data it cannot read. Links expire after 30 days, and every report stops a link being served (D18, D21). Does this count as storage "at the direction of a user"? Does not being able to read the content change our "red flag" position?
   - For child sexual abuse material: the duty to report starts with actual knowledge of "any facts or circumstances" of an apparent violation (18 U.S.C. § 2258A(a)). Reports are a blind takedown: nobody reads the content. Only an abuse-material report carries the full link, which we forward unread to the CyberTipline, with the reporter's written reason when the form asked for one, and preserve. Is forwarding unread enough, and when a tip arrives without a key, what tip is enough? Our plan is to report credible tips promptly and hold the ciphertext for a year after a report (D18). § 2258B bars claims arising from reporting except for intentional misconduct, actual malice, reckless disregard or an unrelated purpose; does that cover a voluntary report of a tip we can't confirm? Does holding only ciphertext, with no key, meet the duty to preserve the "contents" of a report? Given *Doe v. Twitter* (9th Cir. 2025), what makes a reporting process defensible?
4. **Repeat infringers with no accounts.** § 512(i) expects a repeat-infringer policy. Our plan (#1728, owner's strike ladder of 2026-10-20): a strike is two reported links within 90 days, in any category including copyright. Strike 1 pauses link sharing for 1 week, strike 2 for 1 month, and strike 3 ends it permanently. Strikes are recorded with Apple DeviceCheck (two bits per device plus a month-level timestamp), so a strike survives a reinstall. The week and month pauses use a server-signed "sharing paused until" token held by the app, so a reinstall may clear a pause; the next strike still steps up. We never store a device identifier. A paused or ended device can still listen, open links and send `.spokenmargins` files directly (AirDrop, Messages, Files). Is that a reasonable policy, and is it right to treat an install as the "subscriber" when there are no accounts? **Open question: does a ladder that ends in permanent termination satisfy § 512(i)?** Is a week-long first strike too soft for a policy meant to terminate "repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances"?
5. **Defamation, harassment and report duties.** Calling something commentary protects it from a copyright claim, not a defamation claim. We plan a "Report this link" form anyone can reach, a posted Take It Down notice-and-removal process, and a short published content policy (D21). Every report takes the link down at once and nobody reads the content (D21). The sender's app shows only a generic notice, "A link you shared was taken down after a report", the same for every category, with no dispute button and no contact. A sender whose links are reported twice in 90 days takes a strike (question 4). Every report must pick a category (part of the form), a second report from the same reporter within 7 days must also give a written reason, in any category and of any length, and a reporter can be blocked only when their written reasons show they are misusing the button, by a manual decision. Is that process sound? Does a blind takedown with a generic notice meet the EU Digital Services Act's call for diligent, non-arbitrary decisions with a statement of reasons (Arts. 16(6) and 17), and the UK Online Safety Act's complaints duty? Are we a "covered platform" under the Take It Down Act? Does that, with Section 230, meet what we owe the person a comment is about, and the report duties in New York (Gen. Bus. Law § 394-ccc), the EU Digital Services Act (Arts. 16 and 17) and the UK Online Safety Act (s.20)?
6. **What does a single-member LLC cover when I run the service myself?** I'll be the only member, the only operator and the person who acts on notices. Which protections apply to me as well as to the company: the § 512 safe harbors, Section 230, § 2258B, and insurance that names me as an insured? What habits keep the LLC's protection intact? The LLC will be formed in Illinois (D1), and it will carry media liability insurance before the external beta (D19).

## Decided without counsel

Defaults taken on 2026-10-16, and Cole's decisions on 2026-10-20. Veto any by number.

- **D1. An Illinois LLC** (Cole, 2026-10-20). Forming in Delaware or Wyoming adds a second registered agent and a foreign-registration fee, and gives a one-person business run from home nothing it needs. (Inference from general guidance; question 6 can confirm.)
- **D2. Organization developer account under the LLC before the public release.** The D-U-N-S number is free. Moving the app between accounts is easier before launch than after. The server, domain and service accounts move into the LLC's name at the same time (added 2026-10-20, so the LLC visibly runs the service).
- **D3. Register a DMCA designated agent, in the LLC's name, before share-link creation reaches the external TestFlight group, not only before public links** (revised twice on 2026-10-20; see D19). The safe harbor doesn't reach back before registration. $6, renewed every three years ([DMCA Directory FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/faq.html); 37 C.F.R. § 201.38). The agent is the LLC, at a registered-agent or virtual-mailbox address, so Cole's home address stays out of the public directory. The agent's contact details are also posted on our website, as § 512(c)(2) requires.
- **D4. 30-day retention; a report stops the link being served** (revised 2026-10-20; was "deleted on a valid report"). After a takedown we keep the report record (link id, reason, date). The one exception is D18: after a CyberTipline report, the law requires us to hold the content for a year.
- **D5. Sharing terms are accepted at the first share**, as a separate agreement, so the main Terms version doesn't change and nobody else is interrupted. Accepting at the point of use is the usual pattern.
- **D6. The Open RAIL-M voice keeps its tap-to-accept at download**, recording the terms version and date. No custom App Store EULA. Only listeners who download that voice use it, so only they need to agree.
- **D7. Nothing shared today is machine-generated audio.** Shared Margins carry only the listener's own recording or text. If a later feature shares synthesized voice, such as a paid server voice, it is labeled as synthetic; the packet format and the recipient's label for that already exist (`tts_audio_and_text`).
- **D8. Privacy laws.** The CCPA's revenue and volume thresholds are far above ours. For the GDPR, we store nothing linked to a person. The first public App Store release starts on the U.S. storefront and widens after a privacy check; TestFlight testers abroad are fine because nothing about them is stored.
- **D9. App Store privacy label:** declare the App Attest key as Device ID, not linked to the user, used for fraud prevention. That is the conservative reading of Apple's definitions.
- **D10. The listener's own OpenAI key is not a shipped feature.** The code exists but is switched off in every build (`SMRemoteProviderExperienceEnabled: false` in `project.yml`). The privacy policy covers it only if it is ever turned on.
- **D11. Age rating:** answer Apple's questionnaire as written for an app whose shared links anyone can open, and take at least 13+. The app isn't directed at children and has no age gate.
- **D12. A protection service such as Cloudflare is named in the published privacy policy in advance**, so it can be switched on during an attack without a policy change first.
- **D13. The starter podcast list is rebuilt from each show's own feed**, with no Apple chart data or Apple-hosted artwork (#1732). This was Cole's pick on #632.
- **D14. Full transcripts are never shared.** Not with other listeners and not in maps.

Cole's decisions on 2026-10-20 (his words in quotes):

- **D15. Share links never carry the full source text.** "I think we could do this. It will be work for friends wanting to share original work but maybe we can coach them to send the item along with the link to their friend." A packet built for a link drops the embedded source text. Updated the same day: "it was suggested we could just have them send the .spokenmargins file in the message which solves it for me". For an item with no web address, the share flow sends the `.spokenmargins` file, with its full text, in the same message as the link, through Messages or AirDrop, never through our server.
- **D16. Excerpt caps, one rule for the page and the packet.** "I would be fine limiting the amount sent after the margin to 120 characters but would like to keep as much as possible before the commentary." and "how about scaling to the length of the commentary in the margin up to a cap?" Updated the same day: "I like the scale 280 to 800 based on the commentary length", and on a total per link, "Not a fan of this". Starting numbers for the lawyer to mark up, not legal conclusions:
  - after: 120 characters;
  - before: 3 × the comment's length, floor 280, cap 800 characters (spoken Margins: the transcript's length, else about 15 characters per second);
  - no total limit per link; whether one is needed is in question 2.
  - As built (#2237, `ExcerptCapRule`): the before side stops at 500, not 800, because Builds 32 and 34 reject packet context over 500 per side and a new build's packets must import there. The ceiling is one constant, to raise once those builds are retired.
- **D17. Full-text sharing stays in direct file export only** (AirDrop, Messages, Files, with the existing rights confirmation). "deal". It never goes through our server.
- **D18. Report handling.** "we can change these." On any report, the link stops being served (D21). Normal 30-day expiry still applies, except that once a CyberTipline report is filed, the ciphertext, and the full link the abuse-material report form sends, is held for one year in restricted storage (18 U.S.C. § 2258A(h)). We never open it; the CyberTipline and law enforcement do. A credible tip of child sexual abuse material is reported promptly to the CyberTipline. § 2258B bars claims arising from reporting and preservation, except for intentional misconduct, actual malice, reckless disregard or an unrelated purpose; the statute itself doesn't use the words "good faith".
- **D19. The LLC, the DMCA agent and insurance gate the external beta.** First: "Let's register it and make sure it is properly gating." Revised the same day: "with the external beta. Yeah, that's when I'd like to have insurance." Share-link creation may be on for the internal TestFlight group, about five people Cole knows personally. No build with share-link creation switched on goes to the external TestFlight group until all three are true:
  - the Illinois LLC exists (D1);
  - the DMCA designated agent is registered in the LLC's name (Copyright Office account and fee, Cole's own step), and our designated-agent page is live;
  - the LLC has media liability insurance that names its member as an insured and covers user content.
  The gate is set at build time, never by a server switch (the 2026-10-17 rule on gated features).
- **D20. The share page's preview text is encrypted too**, so the server reads nothing it hosts. "I do love privacy. sounds good". The page decrypts in the browser with the key in the link. Message and social previews of a link show only a generic title.
- **D21. A "Report this link" form and a posted Take It Down process.** "I think this is also good". Anyone can reach it, including the person a comment is about, from the share page and from the app. The reporter gets a reply. Settled the same day after three revisions: reports are a blind takedown. "Why would I want access? I can't send it to an AI because it could get me band if the content is disallowed so I would have to either send it to a local model that I can't host or review it myself which I don't want to do. I like the blind take down". Starting numbers for the lawyer to mark up:
  - every report disables the link at once; no person or AI looks at the content;
  - reports carry no key, except in the abuse-material category: the form sends the full link, which we forward unread to the CyberTipline and preserve with the ciphertext for a year (D18);
  - the sender's app shows one generic notice, "A link you shared was taken down after a report". It is the same for every category and never names it, so an abuse-material report looks like any other. There is no "This was a mistake" button, no dispute and no call, and the sender is never contacted personally about a report. This replaces the earlier dispute and call-offer design (owner, 2026-10-20);
  - strike ladder for senders, any category including copyright: a strike is two reported links within 90 days. Strike 1 pauses link sharing for 1 week, strike 2 for 1 month, strike 3 ends it permanently. Direct `.spokenmargins` sharing (AirDrop, Messages, Files) always keeps working; only the server is blocked. This is the repeat-infringer policy for #1728 (question 4). DeviceCheck holds two bits and a month-level timestamp; default: 00 clean, 01 after strike 1, 10 after strike 2, 11 blocked from sharing links and reporting. strikes live in the bits and survive a reinstall; the week and month pauses use a server-signed "sharing paused until" token held by the app, which stores no device identifier, and a reinstall may clear a pause ("that doesn't need to be resiliant to redownloading the app the strikes do");
  - reporters have no unfounded or dispute counts. Every report must pick a category, from the first report on: Cole: "having them have a multiple choice question before a report isn't much of a gate. I think that we can do that immediately" A second report within 7 days from the same phone or reply email requires a written reason before it submits: "if they do a second report in a week, that should be the trigger". This applies to every category; any length is enough, even one character, and the link is disabled the moment the report is filed. Revised the same day: "If we start reporting everything, we are going to be injecting so much noise into them, that we are going to make their job harder" and "I don't think it's too much to say, why we're reporting." A reporter who reports often and gives reasons is welcome. Cole reads the report text, never the reported content. If the reporter seems not to understand the button, we change the design. Refined the same day: a reporter is blocked only when their written reasons show they are misusing the button, and only by Cole's manual decision; "requiring them to write a little note means that it becomes a lot easier to see if they are in the right for abusing the feature." Ignoring or missing the outreach message is never on its own a reason to block. That message is a plain in-app notice from Spoken Margins that says why it was sent, never a pushy "set up a chat" prompt that could look like spam. A reporter whose notes show good-faith reports is never blocked, however many they file;
  - abuse-material and Take It Down reports need the written reason on the same terms as every other category; removal is never delayed, because the link is disabled when the report is filed. In the abuse-material category the reason goes with the tip to the CyberTipline, for context. The sender is never contacted about them;
  - the Terms and the published report policy describe this. A short published content policy covers New York's hateful-conduct law, the EU Digital Services Act Arts. 16 and 17, and the UK Online Safety Act s.20.
