Five Home screens

Every one shows the same four things: one item to continue, at least one to start from your Library, at least one to pull in from your shows, and a Margin worth sharing. They differ in how the eye finds them.

Glanceable icons (same in all five)

LProgress ring: how far you are. Full ring means finished.
FBlue dot: new from a show you follow, not yet in your Library.
S1Orange count: Margins you have on this item.
OPSFaces: friends who listened or left Margins.

1 · Shelves

What Apple Podcasts, Audible and Apple Books do: a Continue hero, then horizontal shelves named by intent. Cover art carries the status icons, so a row can be read in a second. Familiar, scales to any amount of content, and the shelf order is the priority order.

2 · Four tiles

Spotify's shortcut grid, cut to exactly four: Continue, Start, Pull in, Share. One screen, nothing to scroll for, every tile is a 1-tap action. The "More of each" shelf underneath is optional. Best if you want Home to be a launcher, not a feed.

3 · Deck

One card per intent, swiped sideways, with a sentence saying why it's there ("you finish Field Notes episodes more than any other show"). Slowest to scan but the most persuasive; each card has one button. Like Apple News Today or a daily briefing.

4 · Bento

Apple Music's Top Picks and the widget grid: tiles of different sizes, size is importance. Continue is the biggest, Share gets a full-width tile so the quote is readable, Pull in gets two small ones. Dense but still one screen, and the club appears as a thin row only when there is news.

5 · Stories

Instagram's story ring for the feed: one circle per show, ring means unheard, half ring means partly heard, grey means nothing new. That is 60 shows made glanceable in one strip, and it doubles as the way in to Following. Below it, Continue, two Start tiles and the Share card.
